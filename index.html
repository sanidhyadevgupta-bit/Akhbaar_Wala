The brief

संक्षेप

Create a hyper-casual game rooted in Indian context, for Indian people, targeting the users of Dainik Bhaskar's app.DAINIK BHASKAR GAME JAM

Three constraints sit inside that sentence. The game has to be hyper-casual: understood in seconds, played with one mechanic, finished in a minute. It has to feel Indian in its details, not only in its skin. And it has to belong inside a news app, where people arrive to read, not to play.

The third one decided everything. A good answer had to feel like it grew out of the newspaper itself.

Why this game

क्यों

The paperboy is the oldest link between Bhaskar and its readers

For decades the newspaper reached people the same way: rolled up, tossed from a bicycle at dawn, landing on the porch. Before Dainik Bhaskar was an app, it was a bundle on a carrier and a rider who knew every house in the lane.

Instead of inventing a world and attaching the brand to it, I put the player on that cycle. The job of the game is the job of the paper: get the news to every door before sunrise. That gives the brand a reason to exist inside the game, and it makes the game instantly familiar to the people who will play it.

It also opened a door that most jam games don't have: the game can carry the day's real headlines. Every delivery flips a headline in the ticker, and the daily route is framed as "today's edition".

Target audience

पाठक

Designed for the Bhaskar reader, not the typical gamer

Dainik Bhaskar's readers are largely Hindi-first, spread across Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Chhattisgarh, Haryana, Bihar and, through Divya Bhaskar, Gujarat. Many are older than the usual mobile-game player, use budget Android phones, and open the app to read news between other things.

Design process

प्रक्रिया

From eight ideas to a daily habit

  1. Ideate against the brief

    I sketched eight concepts, each built on one tap or swipe and something every Bhaskar reader would recognise. I scored them on brand fit, instant readability, and whether the news could live inside the game.

    Akhbaar WalaCutting ChaiSach ya JhoothGully CricketPatang KaatPani Puri RushTraffic JugaadMandi Taraazu

  2. Prototype the core loop

    A three.js street that generates itself as you ride, with houses on both sides. Saffron rings mark subscribers. Tapping the left or right third throws a paper on a real arc, the middle third hops. Kulhads stand in for lives, and paper bundles on the road refill your stock.

  3. Add screens and juice

    A splash screen with a front-on hero shot, a main menu, pause and game over screens, all styled as front pages and joined by a page-turn wipe. Then the feel: confetti on delivery, a freeze-frame and zoom kick on bullseyes, screen shake and a cracking kulhad on damage, squash and stretch on jumps, and the camera swooping from the menu view to behind the rider.

  4. Fix what playtesting showed

    The first rider read as someone walking, not cycling. His arms hung backwards and his legs swung like a walk cycle. I rebuilt the pose.

    Before: arms angled behind the body, legs swinging back and forth from the hip, no pedals.
    →
    After: seated and leaning over the bars, left hand fixed on the handle, a turning crank, and two-joint legs solved every frame so the feet stay on the pedals.

  5. Build the daily loop, sound and languages

    The brief asked for daily engagement, so the game became a daily edition: a reward streak, a route that is the same for every player that day, three tasks, tips to earn and a shop to spend them in. Alongside it came procedural Indian music, a settings screen, and Hindi, Gujarati and English.

The walking-rider problem showed up by playing on a phone. That became the rule: if something doesn't read at arm's length on a small screen, it changes.

Mechanics

खेल

Three taps, one whole neighbourhood

Scoring and rules

EventEffectWhy
Paper lands in the ring+10The basic success, generous radius so first throws land.
Paper lands dead centre+25A skill ceiling, rewarded with a freeze-frame and gold flash.
4 and 8 deliveries in a row×2, ×3Streaks keep good players tense and give the combo banner a moment.
Paper on a non-subscriber's porch−5Makes aiming matter without ending the run.
Hop clean over a dog or pothole+5Turns a hazard into a small reward.
Miss a subscriber or crash−1 kulhadThree lives, shown as clay cups that crack.
Ride through a bundle+10 papersStart with 15, carry up to 30, so stock is a light resource.
Finish the daily route+200A clear end point that a shared daily score needs.

Speed rises from 9 to 18 metres a second over a run, and the music's tabla speeds up with it. Throws inherit the cycle's speed, so you release before the house, the way a real paperboy does.

Art direction

रूप-रंग

Dawn in a mohalla, printed on newsprint

Two ideas carry the look. The world is the colour of 5:30 in the morning, a violet sky warming to peach. The interface is the newspaper itself: every menu is a front page with a dateline, a city edition and a ₹5 cover price, and moving between screens turns the page.

Sound

All audio is generated in the browser. A tanpura plucks Pa–Sa–Sa–Sa, the tabla plays a keherwa theka, and a bansuri improvises on raag Bhupali. The melody is generated from the date, so every day's edition has its own tune. The tabla follows your speed, quietens in menus and drops out on game over.
